I saw an abstract for a paper called [Fuck Nuance](https://kieranhealy.org/files/papers/fuck-nuance.pdf) by Irish sociologist Kieran Healy:

> Nuance is not a virtue of good sociological theory. Although often demanded and superficially attractive, nuance inhibits the abstraction on which good theory depends. I describe three “nuance traps” common in sociology and show why they should be avoided on grounds of principle, aesthetics, and strategy. The argument is made without prejudice to the substantive heterogeneity of the discipline.

The paper is worth a read but I loved how aggressive and to the point the title was and hearing something clear cut in academia where wishy-washy text thrives.

Funnily enough [I made an explicit call for nuance](/posts/2017-needs-brevity-nuance/) back in 2017 (the same year as Healey wrote his paper):

> [...] Binary constructs need taking down. Sexuality, race, religion, and politics are all fluid ideas. Nuance is necessary in discussing and understanding them. When it comes to racism, it's very important to take subtle differences on board when unravelling the troubles PoC face in their adversity. Intersectionality might seem like a millennial buzzword to you but it's not and needs to be taken seriously. Ignorance of those social modulations lead to further harm and given who's getting inaugurated in a few weeks, we need as much protection as possible.

After reading the paper and reading back what I wrote (after I've stopped cringing about the latter), I realise that I should have used a different word. “Intersectionality” comes with its own baggage but that was at least more specific than “nuanced” and perhaps leans more into the <q>abstraction on which good theory depends</q>. I'm still reading and learning so if I've missed the point, feel free to let me know.
