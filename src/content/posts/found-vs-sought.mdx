A few months ago, I started logging into Pinterest daily. I didn't know what I was looking for but I knew I wanted the images to make me feel good. My interests vary and I have a lot of curated boards for things like [colours](/jardim/colours/), [Black cultures](/jardim/black/), [tech](/jardim/tech/), [sport](/jardim/sport/), album cover inspo, house stuff, great photography, swag animals—all sorts!

But after a while, I realised that the feed and my boards weren't as much about cool stuff I found and more algorithmically-influenced selections. Sure, I was the one curating them as I wasn't just putting anything and everything onto my boards but it was almost entirely made up of what Pinterest showed me based on what I'd picked. You know how these algorithms go!

And that's why I had to stop going on it so frequently. I started removing pins from the recommendation feed to try and reduce bias (as much as you can with these things) but the more you interact with, the more you're playing whack-a-mole and it kills the enjoyment of the process. I was seeking out the inspiration rather than allowing myself to find it. That's not to say you must sit and let things come to you. It's a creative tug-of-war like most things in my brain but damn seeking content has hands.

[Are.na](https://www.are.na/) feels better in that regard as what you get is based on what the people you follow add to their channels. There's an argument that they may fall under the same spell as me with the algo slant but it's more diluted when it gets to me as I'll still be giving things a thumbs up or down like some Roman emperor who moonlights as a digital content curator. I'm trying to imagine [Commodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodus) making a moodboard while laying on his bed, kicking his legs behind him. But I digress (massively).
